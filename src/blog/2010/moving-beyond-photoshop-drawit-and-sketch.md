I started doing web design with Adobe Photoshop even before Adobe bought Macromedia back in 2005. For years the industry workflow was basically the same for almost everyone: Illustrator for icons, logos and anything vector, and Photoshop for laying out full websites.

It was so common that nobody really questioned it. Even if Photoshop was born for photo retouching and Illustrator for print, we all learned our shortcuts, built our PSD templates and sliced images for the browser.

Lately though, especially after the iPhone 4 came out with the Retina display, I am starting to think Photoshop and Illustrator are not the ideal choice for web and interface design anymore.

## The @2x problem with raster workflows

Until a few months ago, designing everything at `@1x` in Photoshop felt mostly fine, as long as you were careful with pixel alignment. Now with the Retina display, `@1x` is not enough anymore. To be safe and make sure icons, buttons and interfaces stay sharp, it makes much more sense to set up projects at `@2x` or keep everything purely vector so you can scale cleanly.

Doing that in Photoshop feels awkward pretty quickly. Files get huge, layer styles and bitmap shapes don't always scale nicely, and you spend way too much time fighting a photo editor just to align boxes and export clean UI assets.

## Paying a lot for heavy tools we barely use

The other thing that bothers me more and more is how bloated Creative Suite has become. Photoshop and Illustrator are slow to launch, heavy on memory, and full of features for print, CMYK, photo manipulation and 3D that web designers almost never touch.

And we are paying a lot of money for all of it. Buying or upgrading Adobe Creative Suite costs hundreds of dollars, mostly to pay for huge features we don't even need in our day to day web work. It is kind of crazy when you stop and think about it.

## Why DrawIt and Sketch make me excited

That is why I have been really excited to follow smaller, lightweight apps built specifically as native tools for Mac OS X.

A while ago I started keeping an eye on DrawIt by Bohemian Coding. It was fast, vector based, clean and felt right at home on the Mac without all the extra noise of Adobe's interface. And now they just took that idea further and launched Sketch last month.

Of course it is still very early days and Photoshop is not disappearing tomorrow, especially when clients and agencies still expect PSD files. Still, seeing a lightweight, native Mac vector app focused on clean digital graphics makes a lot of sense to me. Working natively in vectors makes `@1x` and `@2x` workflows way less painful, and the app actually feels fast when you use it.

In my opinion, dedicated lightweight tools like Sketch are going to be the future for web design work. We don't need giant print and photo suites anymore just to design screens.
